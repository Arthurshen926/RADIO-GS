\begin{abstract}
Refining a scene-query response can improve teacher agreement while damaging
an already correct answer. We study when an existing complete response should
be retained or replaced. Frozen providers supply an incumbent and intact
alternatives; query--response signatures describe their observable agreement.
A shared utility estimator and one calibrated margin choose a whole answer,
without retraining teacher representations or complete membership decoders.
We organize evaluation around five outputs: LERF semantic segmentation and
projected 3D selection, ScanNet open-vocabulary segmentation, NVOS and multimodal
UQIS. Published comparisons and previously verified local reproductions establish
the reference context. Matched mechanism controls currently cover ten ScanNet
rooms and all 268 UQIS queries. The latest shared policy raises UQIS image set
IoU from 39.90 to 40.99\%, with one improved query and no harmed queries, while
preserving the other scored outputs. Removing its null-query constraint yields
identical decisions, and LERF/NVOS transfer remains open. The resulting evidence
separates useful candidate capacity from ranking error and the cost of retention;
it does not yet establish universal improvement across the five outputs.
\end{abstract}
